About me

I am an observational astrophysicist working on supernova remnants (SNRs): the expanding shells of gas left behind when stars explode.

SNRs return heavy elements and energy to the interstellar medium, heat and shape it, and can trigger new star formation. Studying whole populations of them in other galaxies lets us see how this feedback depends on the environment. My work combines optical imaging and integral-field spectroscopy with X-ray data and models of shock emission.

I did my PhD at the University of Crete, and from 2022 to 2026 I was a postdoctoral fellow at the Institute of Space Sciences (ICE‑CSIC) in Barcelona, with María de Maeztu and Juan de la Cierva fellowships. Along the way I have observed at La Silla, Cerro Tololo, Skinakas and Helmos, and spent research stays in Granada, Palermo and at the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian.

Research

My main question: how do supernova remnants evolve, and how do they shape the galaxies they live in? To answer it, we first need large, complete and unbiased samples, so much of my work is about finding them.

Diagnosticsidentification

New tools to find supernova remnants

The classic test, [S II]/Hα > 0.4, misses many remnants, especially young or fast-shock ones. I develop multi-line diagnostics based on shock and photoionization models, and machine-learning classifiers for emission-line nebulae, that separate SNRs from H II regions and other nebulae more completely.

Populationsnearby galaxies

Extragalactic SNR populations

With integral-field spectroscopy (MUSE/VLT, WEAVE/WHT) and narrow-band imaging, I build large SNR samples and measure their densities, shock velocities and luminosity functions. In NGC 7793 this raised the number of known SNRs to about 240; current targets include the starburst galaxy IC 342.

Multi‑λoptical + X‑ray

X-ray counterparts

Combining Chandra and XMM‑Newton archives with optical catalogues to find X-ray SNRs beyond the Local Group, and relating their X-ray emission to the density of their surroundings.

Modelstheory & HPC

Luminosity functions and simulations

Models of optical emission-line luminosity functions for SNR populations, and hydrodynamic simulations of shocks interacting with dense clouds, such as in Calvera's high-latitude remnant.

Transientsspectroscopy

Novae and supernovae

A quantitative method to follow the spectral-class evolution of classical novae, the peculiar 1991bg-like Type Ia SN 2023vjh, and supernova follow-up within the ePESSTO collaboration.
